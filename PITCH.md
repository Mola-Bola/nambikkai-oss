# Nambikkai — the pitch
_One page. For the skeptical, including future us._

## The observation

AI journaling is a crowded, funded category — chat-based mentors, thinking partners,
mood trackers. Read the leaders closely and they share two properties: your inner life
lives on their cloud, and the record is a *diary of events*. Nobody keeps receipts on
**beliefs** — what you concluded about a person or yourself, when you concluded it, and
whether you'd still sign it today.

Meanwhile the strain that actually costs people isn't the event. It's the stale
appraisal: a truth formed in one bad week, held for years, compounding quietly.

## The origin

Nambikkai began life as the trust layer of a working personal life-OS — a redaction
perimeter, bitemporal provenance for facts, a golden test corpus. The pivot came from
living with it. Its builder moved countries, missed home, and watched the provenance
discipline surface something uncomfortable: beliefs about people, formed long ago and
never revisited, had quietly strained real relationships. A tool built to keep
receipts on *facts* turned out to matter most pointed at *feelings*.

## The thesis

**Held truths compound like debt; provenance is how you audit them.** Appraisal theory
says feelings follow beliefs, and revising the belief revises the feeling. A journal
whose entries carry *valid-from / valid-to* is reappraisal with receipts — "what I
believed then" beside "what I know now", unwoven when the person is ready, shared (if
ever) only on their word.

## Why it's defensible

1. **The appraisal ledger.** No shipped competitor does belief-revision provenance.
   The bitemporal engine already exists in this repo and runs in production — the moat
   is a working mechanism plus the conviction that a journal is a *ledger*, not a chat.

2. **Privacy as architecture, not marketing.** Local-first storage, people-by-role,
   identity non-retention, hash-chained entries — inherited from a production trust
   layer, on from the first entry. The category leaders market encryption on their
   cloud; we don't have a cloud to breach. For the most personal dataset in software,
   that's not a feature — it's the brand.

3. **The reflection loop is the effect-size moderator.** Expressive writing shows
   real but modest effects; meta-analytically, *feedback roughly doubles them*. The
   weekly reflection — gentle, self-distancing, never diagnostic — is that moderator,
   built as the product's heart. Capture alone is already regulating (affect
   labelling); the loop is where compounding happens.

4. **The honesty discipline.** The trust layer's corpus culture carries over: every
   guard golden-tested, every blind spot published (`known_gap`), every incident a
   permanent case. A journal asks for more trust than any dev-tool; this is how it's
   earned rather than claimed.

## The lines we do not cross

**A journal, never therapy.** No diagnostic or treatment language anywhere — copy,
docs, replies. Distress in the record earns a warm nudge toward distance or a human,
never AI counselling. (This is both the law's direction — Illinois's WOPR Act and kin
— and the voice the product wants anyway.)

**The ethics gate on data.** Calibration only against licensed research corpora and
explicitly-published writing; no scraping; nothing regurgitated; user entries never
leave the local spine for training. The builder's own ledger included.

## Proof it's real

The privacy spine isn't a pitch-deck promise: the perimeter has blocked live leaks in
production (including this project's own test file), the corpus replays every past
incident on every run, and the journal's capture path shipped with its goldens —
routing, schema, name-to-role swap, and chain tamper-evidence — the day it was built.

## The shape of the bet

Telegram-first MVP on the builder's own life-OS rails; the builder is user #1. The
product generalises outward from one demonstrably-served life, not inward from a
persona. Habit tracking joins only after the journal loop earns daily use — and it
will reward *returning after a gap* over streaks, because the person the product
exists for is the one having the bad week.

## The team

platforms) and their agents, building from a system they actually live in.
